What it does

Built for a community pharmacy counter, not a hospital IT department.

Medicine database

81 medicines across 71 therapeutic classes, each with molecular formula, manufacturer and drug licence number, Indian Pharmacopoeia and BP/USP monograph references, mechanism of action, pharmacokinetics, pregnancy category, and HSN/GST and schedule classification.

Patients and allergies

Allergy tracking that understands within-class cross-reactivity - a recorded penicillin allergy correctly blocks amoxicillin - plus weight, serum creatinine, hepatic and renal status, and pregnancy or lactation.

Weight-based dosing

Paediatric doses calculated from published mg/kg figures where they exist, falling back to Young's rule and labelling the confidence honestly. Every result is capped at the maximum single dose, so a heavy child cannot be handed an adult overdose.

Substitutions

When the first choice is out of stock: same-molecule swaps first, then genuine therapeutic alternatives ranked by how little clinical substitution is needed. Anything unsafe for the patient is excluded, not merely warned about.

Administration cycles

Course length, dose interval, clock times, tapering requirements and missed-dose advice, derived per drug class - so a 5-day antibiotic and a 5-day steroid are not printed as if they were the same thing.

Printed reports

Prescriptions laid out in the classical pharmacopoeial order - superscription, inscription, subscription, signatura - with the statutory Schedule H1 red-box warning, prescriber registration number and dual signature blocks. Your pharmacy name and logo on every sheet.

Inventory

Stock levels, batch and expiry tracking, low-stock alerts, and valuation in rupees. Out-of-stock lines feed the substitution engine, so an empty shelf produces an answer rather than a shrug.

Backups

Automatic snapshots on every launch, plus one-click export and restore. Your records live outside the program folder, so upgrading, reinstalling or moving the app never touches patient data.

It refuses to guess

Most software tells you what you asked for. This tells you what it can prove - and stays quiet about the rest.

Every recommendation passes one gate

The safety engine is deliberately pessimistic. It starts from not proven safe and only relaxes when a check actively passes. A missed contraindication harms a patient; a missed sale costs money. The asymmetry is intentional.

Five verdicts, not a yes/no

  • Contraindicated Do not dispense. There is no override path.
  • Not safe A serious risk for this patient.
  • Caution Pharmacist review needed. Never auto-recommended.
  • Safe Every check passed. The only auto-recommended verdict.
  • Cannot verify A required check could not run. Treated as unsafe.

What it checks

Allergy and cross-reactivity · comorbidity conflicts · serious drug interactions · Beers Criteria for the over-65s · paediatric age limits · pregnancy and lactation · renal dosing by eGFR · hepatic impairment · dose ceilings · form and unit consistency.

Windows SmartScreen

The installer is not code-signed, so Windows may show a SmartScreen warning on first run. Click More info, then Run anyway. Code signing requires a paid certificate.

Where your data lives

Your records are stored at %LOCALAPPDATA%\PharmMS\pharmacy.db - deliberately outside the program folder. Upgrading, reinstalling or moving the application never touches patient data, and uninstalling asks before deleting anything.

Questions

Does it really work with no internet?

Yes. The application makes no internet requests of its own - no CDN fonts, no analytics, no telemetry, no cloud sync. It runs normally with networking completely disabled.

One optional feature can use the network: a drug reference lookup that pulls label data from the FDA's public database. It is off unless you turn it on, sends nothing but the drug name, caches every result so it keeps working offline afterwards, and is never fed into the recommendation engine. If it cannot reach the network it tells you plainly, rather than showing an empty result that looks like "no warnings".

Do I need administrator rights?

No. It installs to your own user folder and registers itself under Apps & features so you can uninstall it normally.

What happens to my records when I upgrade?

Nothing. Your database lives outside the program folder, and the installer brings the schema forward in place - adding new columns without touching existing rows. There is a test that proves this against a populated database, because losing patient records during an upgrade would be unacceptable.

Is this a substitute for a pharmacist or prescriber?

No, and it does not pretend to be. It is a record-keeping and decision-support tool. Every calculated dose is an estimate to be verified by a qualified prescriber. Verify medicine data against the current edition of the relevant pharmacopoeia and the manufacturer's labelling before relying on it.

What it will do is refuse to recommend something it cannot verify, and tell you what to record so it can. Absence of a warning is not evidence of safety - the interaction tables are curated and therefore incomplete by nature.

Can I put my own pharmacy name and logo on it?

Yes. Go to the Branding tab, enter your pharmacy name and licence key, and upload your logo and address, phone, drug licence number and GSTIN. Your details then appear on the app's startup screen and on every printed prescription and patient report.

Is the source code available?

Yes - github.com/storm-21/pharms. The README covers building from source, the test suites, and adding medicines in bulk from a CDSCO or Jan Aushadhi catalogue.
